I type a lot. Emails, Slack messages, docs, code comments, PRs. And I kept noticing the same thing: my thoughts move faster than my fingers. By the time I finish typing a sentence, I've already lost the next three.

So I built Bolo — a native macOS menu bar app that turns your voice into clean, ready-to-use text in any application. No window to switch to. No app to open. Just hold a key, talk, and let go.

What it looks like in practice

You're writing an email. You hold Ctrl+Shift. You say:

What you say
"Hey Sarah, thanks for sending over the Q3 numbers. I had a quick look and everything checks out. Let's, um, actually, can we set up a call on Thursday to walk through the projections? I want to make sure we're aligned before the board meeting."
↓
What appears
Hey Sarah, thanks for sending over the Q3 numbers. I had a quick look and everything checks out. Can we set up a call on Thursday to walk through the projections? I want to make sure we're aligned before the board meeting.

The "um" disappeared, the "let's, actually" course-correction was handled, punctuation and capitalization are correct. The whole thing took about 2 seconds.

Three modes

Push-to-Talk

Ctrl+Shift

Hold the key, speak a sentence or two, release. Text appears instantly. This is what I use 90% of the time.

Long-Talk

Ctrl+Shift+Space

Toggle extended recording. Brain dump into an email, a doc, or meeting notes without touching the keyboard.

Command Mode

Select text + Ctrl+Shift

Speak a command: "make this more formal", "fix the grammar", "translate to Spanish." Bolo rewrites the selected text in place.

How it works

The architecture is surprisingly straightforward. Three steps:

1

Capture

When you hold the hotkey, AVAudioEngine starts recording through your Mac's microphone. Raw PCM audio accumulates in a buffer. When you release, it wraps everything in a WAV header.

2

Process

The audio gets base64-encoded and sent to Google Gemini's multimodal API. The prompt instructs Gemini to clean up filler words, add punctuation, handle corrections, and match the tone based on surrounding text context.

3

Insert

The transcribed text gets inserted at your cursor using macOS Accessibility APIs. The app detects which app you're in, finds the focused text field, and types the text directly — even re-activating the correct app if focus shifted during processing.

The trickiest part was hotkey detection. The app installs a CGEvent tap — a low-level macOS mechanism that intercepts keyboard events before they reach any app. This is the only way to get system-wide key detection that works in every application.

The small details

Personal dictionary

Teach Bolo your colleague names, product names, and jargon. "Kubernetes" comes out right instead of "Cooper Netties."

Context window

Reads text around your cursor so Gemini knows if you're in a bulleted list, a paragraph, or a reply thread.

Course correction

Say "no wait" or "actually" mid-sentence and Bolo keeps only what you meant.

Word counter

Daily count in the menu bar. All-time stats in the history view. A small thing, but satisfying.

Enterprise ready

Need per-user token tracking for your company? Bolo supports Vertex AI with OAuth2 — users sign in with their Google Workspace account, and every API call carries their identity. GCP admins can track usage, set quotas, and manage access through standard IAM roles. The OAuth2 flow uses Authorization Code with PKCE — no client secret needed.
